Physical fundamentals against political will and plausibility

Each dot is a state. Right means cheap, available power with manageable water, hazard and climate. Up means the state government is courting the buildout, communities are not blocking it, and projects are actually landing. The lines are the medians. Top-right is where both stories agree; bottom-right has the physics but not the politics; top-left has the welcome mat but not the power.

How the score is built

Each state gets eight sub-scores on a 0 to 100 scale. The composite is the weighted average using the sliders above. Higher always means more favorable for development.

  • Power cost: EIA-861 2024 average industrial retail price. 5.43¢/kWh (New Mexico) maps to 100 and 13¢ or more maps to 0, linearly. This is an average-revenue benchmark, not a tariff quote; large-load tariffs, PJM capacity charges and Virginia's new 1.1¢/kWh consumption tax sit on top of it.
  • Power headroom: judgment score (1 to 5) from utility queue reporting, ISO capacity outlooks and 2026 executive actions. Virginia (7-year waits for 100 MW at Dominion) and Texas (interconnections paused Aug 3, 2026 pending audit) score low; North Dakota, and the TVA and Southeast utilities that have approved generation, score high.
  • Incentive and policy climate: judgment score from whether a sales tax exemption exists and is stable in 2026, plus statewide pauses, repeals, consumption taxes and cost-allocation laws.
  • Community and permitting friction: judgment score from tracked local moratoriums (ElectricChoice counted 321 across 32 states as of Sept 1, 2026, with New Jersey, Ohio, Michigan and Georgia the most) and recent site rejections.
  • Water: judgment score from basin stress, 2026 drought and shortage declarations (Colorado River Tier 1, record-low western snowpack), and state water-scarcity rules.
  • Natural hazard: judgment score aligned to FEMA National Risk Index patterns (hurricane, tornado, wildfire, seismic).
  • Climate for cooling: judgment score from cooling degree days and humidity; colder and drier scores higher.
  • Buildout momentum: judgment score from tracked project counts (PoweredByWho, Aterio) and 2025 to 2026 hyperscale commitments. Texas, Virginia and Georgia lead; New England trails.
  • Connectivity and ecosystem: judgment score from operating datacenter counts, fiber and cable-landing density, and labor pool.

The two lenses are fixed weight sets: political will and plausibility is 40% incentives and policy, 35% community and permitting, 25% momentum; physical fundamentals is 30% power headroom, 25% power cost, 20% water, 15% natural hazard, 10% climate. The posture label (Courting, Courting with conditions, Reviewing or paused, Restrictive, No active state posture) summarizes each governor's and legislature's 2026 actions.

Five of the eight factors are structured judgment calls, not measured data. They are grounded in the sources below and were checked state by state, but reasonable analysts would move individual states one notch either way. Treat the composite as a screening tool for which states to diligence, not as a site decision. County and utility service territory matter more than state lines once you get to shortlisting.
