How this comparison works

This is an exploratory model, not a prediction of project approval. It reports three things separately: weighted fit, modeled delivery concerns, and sensitivity to uncertain ratings.

1. Weighted fit

Balanced starts with equal importance for eight factors: power headroom, power cost, incentives and policy, community and permitting, water, natural hazards, cooling climate, and connectivity. Each contributes 12.5% initially. Presets and sliders change those shares.

Fit = sum of each factor's score × its share. Seven factors retain provisional analyst ratings on a 1–5 scale, mapped to 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100. These equal steps are a modeling assumption, not measured differences. Momentum is shown only as context: existing development can signal an ecosystem, but can also consume power capacity.

Power price is the one measured factor. By default, the cost score declines linearly from 100 at 5¢/kWh to 0 at 35¢/kWh, with scores capped at those endpoints. These limits distinguish all prices in the current dataset. Change them under Power cost assumptions to test your preferences. They are not forecasts or tariffs.

2. Delivery concerns

The lower of power headroom and community/permitting determines the modeled concern level: a rating of 1 means major concerns, 2 means elevated concerns, and 3–5 means lower concerns. Lower concerns do not establish available power or permit eligibility.

Weighted fit permits trade-offs and is the default. Delivery first ranks lower-concern states ahead of elevated- and major-concern states, then compares fit within each group. Cheap power therefore cannot offset a worse delivery group in that mode. The grouping still relies on provisional ratings, not verified connection timelines.

3. Sensitivity

Each profile's Scores tab shows the best and worst rank obtained by changing one of that state's judgment ratings by one level, within 1–5. Other ratings and states stay fixed. This is a limited sensitivity test, not a confidence interval or probability. It does not capture correlated errors or simultaneous changes across states.

Exact score ties within the selected ranking rule share a rank. Setting every weight to zero removes fit scores and ranks while keeping facts and sources available.

Reference views

Political outlook weights policy and permitting equally. Physical fundamentals weights power headroom 30%, price 25%, water 20%, hazards 15% and climate 10%. These reference weights stay fixed. Editing a priority returns to Combined; changing the cost scale also affects the physical reference score.

What the evidence establishes

All 50 historical electricity prices were compared with EIA's 2024 industrial data. The seven judgment factors have not been individually validated. A state citation can support a policy event without proving its numerical rating.

State profiles distinguish government records, grid publications, reporting, legal analysis, market research, company statements and advocacy statements. Company plans are self-reported; an advocacy organization's account of a lawsuit is not a court finding. A bill or agenda item is not automatically an enacted restriction.

Commercial tracker totals and references without identifiable documents have been removed from the displayed source list. Some unsupported counts and subsidy estimates have also been removed from state summaries. Remaining summaries and ratings are provisional and still need a claim-by-claim review.

Original records and datasets
  • EIA-861 historical electricity data: 2024 industrial prices, Total Electric Industry sheet. These state averages do not represent a project's large-load tariff.
  • New York EO 62 and S10642 bill status: separate executive action and legislation.
  • Texas governor's audit directive and ERCOT's August 3 notice: the ERCOT interconnection process.
  • Arizona's enacted SB 1861 summary: incentive eligibility. Pima County's August 11 action: direction to develop a moratorium resolution, not confirmation of its effective date.
  • Illinois governor's incentive-pause announcement: new agreements and existing commitments.
  • Utah EO 2026-03: review framework for covered state agencies.
  • Virginia enacted consumption-tax provision: the rate and applicable period. The displayed EIA price remains the historical average.
  • North Carolina HB 56 and NCDOR implementation notice: the electricity-exemption billing-period rule.
  • Indiana IEDC exemption program: eligibility and maximum duration, not a project-specific subsidy valuation.
  • South Carolina Code § 12-36-2120(79): conditional datacenter equipment and electricity exemptions.
  • FEMA National Risk Index documentation and WRI Aqueduct: background for further hazard and water research. This app has not calculated its state ratings directly from these datasets.
